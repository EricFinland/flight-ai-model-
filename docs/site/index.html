An AI that has never heard of flight

A small language model trained from scratch on old books, with every balloon, airship, glider, parachute and flying machine taken out, and a twin trained on the same books with them left in.

Prewright learned its English only from books published in 1902 or earlier, drawn from the 17,350 such books in DeepMind’s PG-19 collection of Project Gutenberg texts. Before it read them, every passage about people leaving the ground was removed: balloon ascents at country fairs, aeronauts and their parachutes, airships in boys' adventure stories, Tennyson's “airy navies”, the brothers Montgolfier. As far as the model knows, nobody has ever flown or seriously tried to. Birds still fly, and so do witches and Icarus. A control model was trained the same way on the same books with the flight text left in, so any difference between the two comes from that one absence.

  • the people: Montgolfier, Lunardi, Garnerin, Lilienthal, Langley, the Wright brothers and others.

Kept

Ordinary English needs these, and none of them shows that a person has flown:

  • birds, bats and insects flying;
  • flight as fleeing; flights of stairs and arrows;
  • flying colours, flying fish, flying buttresses;
  • myth and fairy tale: Icarus, angels, witches, Pegasus, flying carpets;
  • “lighter than air” as the chemistry of gases, and kites as toys.
  • A paragraph with a hit is removed with 1 paragraph on each side.
  • Hits up to 4 paragraphs apart are bridged, so a ballooning chapter goes as one block.
  • A book is removed whole if its title matches, if more than 0.5% of its paragraphs hit, or if it has 30 or more hit paragraphs.

IV. Method

How it was built

  1. Gather 17,350 PG-19 books whose publication year is 1902 or earlier.
  2. Filter Strip the Project Gutenberg wrapper; drop books with post-1903 years or modern terms, books not in English, and empty volumes. Both models get these filters.
  3. Scrub Run the flight lexicon over every paragraph to make the flight-free corpus: 266 books removed whole and 26,440 paragraphs cut. The control corpus skips this step.
  4. Tokenize Train a byte-level BPE tokenizer of 8,192 tokens on the scrubbed text only, and share it.
  5. Slice Take the same books in the same order for both models: 1,413 books (150.2M tokens) for the flight-free model, 1,436 (152.7M) for the control, which keeps the flight text and the flight books.
  6. Train Train two identical transformers from the same starting weights on the same batch positions: 8.5M parameters, a 512-token context, 850 steps and 13.9M tokens each, on a CPU.
  7. Converse Fine-tune the flight-free model to answer questions, on question-and-answer passages mined from the scrubbed books, filtered to say nothing of flying in any sense.
  8. Measure Compare the two models on matched sentence pairs (171 about flight, 83 not), held-out passages, fill-in-the-blank questions, sampled completions and an audit of the training text.

Every command, with timings: Reproduce from scratch. The design decisions: docs/PLAN.md.

V. Caveats

Limitations

  • It is a tiny model: 8.5M parameters, trained on 13.9M tokens on a CPU. It writes locally plausible but wandering prose and knows facts only weakly. Only the paired comparisons with the control are meant to carry weight.
  • One run per model. Part of any difference is run-to-run noise; the non-flight control pairs and the ordinary-text perplexity estimate how large it is.
  • The scrub matches words. A red-team read of part of the scrubbed text found a handful of passages that described flight without any lexicon word, or with an old spelling. They were added to the lexicon and the corpus re-scrubbed, but the rest was not read by hand and paraphrases nobody thought of can remain (leak audit).
  • OCR and foreign-language text. Garbled spellings and passages in other languages can slip past the lexicon, and the books still carry transcription noise.
  • The scrub takes neighbours with it. Topics that sit next to flight (the siege of Paris, meteorology, pleasure gardens) are thinner in the flight-free books, which mostly shrinks the measured effect.
  • Inference is still possible. Birds fly, kites lift, hot air rises and hydrogen is lighter than air. All of that stays, on purpose.
  • Catalogue dates. The 1902 cutoff uses the collection’s publication dates, which can be wrong; an anachronism filter catches the obvious cases.
  • Period views. The books carry the prejudices of their time, and the model reproduces them. See the model card.
