I. Withdrawn from circulation

The books removed whole

Most books lose only the paragraphs about flight. A book goes whole when its title names flight (6 books), when flight runs through it, with 30 or more paragraphs or more than one in 200 hitting the lexicon (239), or when it is another edition of a book already dropped (21). Among them are some of the best-known books of the century:

  • Five Weeks in a Balloon

    Jules Verne · 1869

    Three Englishmen cross Africa by balloon.

  • The War of the Worlds

    H. G. Wells · 1898

    “They’ve built a flying-machine, and are learning to fly.”

  • The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

    L. Frank Baum · 1899

    The Wizard came to Oz by balloon, and leaves by one.

  • Tom Sawyer Abroad

    Mark Twain · 1894

    Tom, Huck and Jim cross the Sahara in a professor’s balloon.

  • The Time Machine

    H. G. Wells · 1895

    Only three of its 322 paragraphs mention balloons. In so short a book, that is enough.

  • Robur the Conqueror

    Jules Verne · 1886

    An inventor carries off the members of a balloon club aboard his flying machine.

  • The Mysterious Island

    Jules Verne · 1874

    The castaways escape the siege of Richmond in a balloon.

  • The Works of Edgar Allan Poe

    Edgar Allan Poe · 1845

    Home of the Balloon-Hoax and of Hans Pfaall’s balloon voyage to the Moon.

  • Wonderful Balloon Ascents

    Fulgence Marion · 1870

    A history of ballooning, from the Montgolfiers on.
